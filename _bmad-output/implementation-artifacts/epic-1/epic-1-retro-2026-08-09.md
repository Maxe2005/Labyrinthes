## Epic Summary

Epic 1 established the structural foundation for the `rewrite` branch: the immutable domain model, the two persistence ports and their concrete implementations, the shared Tkinter design-token/widget toolkit, the single composition root and screen router, Home's breadcrumb navigation and Settings access, the end-to-end theme toggle, and the accessibility/keyboard-shortcut floor.

- **Stories:** 10/10 done (1.1 → 1.10)
- **Review findings across stories 1.3–1.10:** 32 patched in-review, 37 deliberately deferred to `deferred-work.md`, 43 rejected as noise
- **`intent_gap` / `bad_spec` findings:** 0 across all 8 spec-driven review passes (1.3–1.10)
- **Highest-severity findings:** 2 `high` findings, both in Story 1.10 (the last, widest-surface story), both fixed pre-merge

## What Went Well

- **Zero intent/spec-quality findings across 8 consecutive review passes.** No review ever concluded "this doesn't match the epic's intent" or "the spec itself is wrong" — the upstream spec-writing step is well-calibrated to what review actually needs.
- **Story 1.2 caught its own blind spot and fixed it durably.** The domain/UI boundary scanner's tests were tautologically green (nothing existed yet for them to catch), so two real resolution bugs slipped through two review iterations before being reproduced manually. The fix wasn't a patch — it was a dedicated fixture-based regression suite (`test_architecture_boundaries_scanner.py`) proving the scanner catches synthetic violations. That protected every one of the following 8 stories' boundary enforcement.
- **Scope precedents were set once and then reliably reused.** Once "no runtime type validation without a static type-checker" was adjudicated in Story 1.3, every later review correctly cited and rejected the same class of finding instead of re-litigating it — a real efficiency gain visible in the reject counts (7–10 per story on this exact class through 1.3–1.5).
- **The deferred-work ledger worked at least once as designed.** Story 1.8 explicitly deferred "`Theme.LIGHT` hardcoded in 5 places" with a note for Story 1.9's implementer to consolidate it — and Story 1.9 did exactly that.
- **Emerging patterns were reused, not reinvented.** Test-fixture hoisting (Story 1.7's shared `tk_root`, Story 1.8's `navigate_stub`/`find_all`) shows the same duplication-reduction instinct applied consistently story over story.

## Challenges (systemic, no blame)

- **The same architectural gap was independently rediscovered three times without ever being fixed.** `Router.navigate()`'s frame-destroy silently cascades to close an open `SettingsWindow`. Deferred in Story 1.8 ("nothing stateful to lose yet"), re-deferred in Story 1.9 (same reasoning), re-deferred again in Story 1.10. The deferred-work ledger surfaced the problem correctly each time — but nothing in the process forces a "deferred N times" item to graduate into a scheduled fix.
- **The same non-atomic-write and untyped-exception gaps recurred and escalated.** `write_maze_csv`/`write_setting_value` have no temp-file-plus-rename safety, and malformed persisted content raises a raw `json.JSONDecodeError`/`IndexError` instead of a typed `LabyrinthesError`. Deferred in Story 1.4, mirrored identically in Story 1.5, and materially escalated in Story 1.9, where `ThemeController` became the first consumer that reads settings *unconditionally on every app launch* — turning a narrow, hard-to-hit edge case into something on the cold-start path.
- **Story 1.10 needed a follow-up review pass with the epic's only two `high`-severity findings** (a shortcut silently wiped by re-navigate-to-self ordering; Shift+key never firing despite the printed uppercase label). Not a fault of the story — it's the natural cost of the last story in an epic touching every widget and every screen — but worth naming as a pattern: the widest-surface story in an epic is inherently higher-risk and could budget for a follow-up pass upfront.

## Decisions Made in This Retrospective

Max reviewed both recurring debt threads and chose to close them out **before** Epic 2 starts, rather than deferring further:

1. **Fix the `Router.navigate()` → `SettingsWindow`-cascade gap before Epic 2.** Rationale: Epic 2's gameplay screen (Story 2.4) introduces real stateful UI (HUD, timer, position) — the "nothing to lose yet" justification that carried this deferral through three stories no longer holds once Epic 2 lands.
2. **Add a dedicated persistence-hardening story before Epic 2**, covering atomic writes (temp-file-plus-rename) and typed `LabyrinthesError`s for malformed/corrupt content in both `MazeRepository` and `SettingsRepository`, isolated from any single feature story rather than folded into one.

No discoveries from Epic 1 require changing Epic 2's *scope or story list* — these two items are additive prep work, not a re-plan.
